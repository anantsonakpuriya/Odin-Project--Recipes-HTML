Description

Pizza is one of India's most beloved comfort foods, pairing a spicy, tangy chickpea curry (chole) with a fluffy, deep-fried leavened bread (bhatura). Originating in the northern region, this iconic duo has evolved from a traditional Punjabi staple into a wildly popular nationwide street food.

Chole bhature; also known as channa bhatura is a dish pairing chickpea curry with bhatura, a deep-fried flatbread. A common street food, chole bhature is often eaten as a breakfast dish. It is associated with Punjabi cuisine, though various views exist on the dish's origin. It is also popular in Delhi, where it was introduced after the partition of India. By the 2010s, it had become a popular fast food across India. The Indo-Trinidadian dish doubles is controversially said to be an adaptation of chole bhature.

Ingredients

For the Chhole(Chickpeas)

  • Kabuli Chana: 2 cups (soaked overnight or for 7–8 hours)
  • Loose Tea Leaves:1 tbsp tied in a muslin cloth (or 1 cup of brewed tea water to give that classic dark Delhi-style color)
  • Whole Spices:1 bay leaf (tejpatta), 1 black cardamom (badi elaichi), 1 cinnamon stick (dalchini), 3 cloves (laung)
  • Spice Powders: 2 tbsp Chole masala, 1 tbsp Kashmiri red chili powder, 1/2 tsp turmeric powder, 1/2 tsp amchur (dry mango powder) or anardana (pomegranate) powder
  • Baking Soda: 1/4 tsp (added while boiling to make chickpeas buttery soft)

For Bhature

  • Maida (All-Purpose Flour): 2 cups
  • Sooji (Semolina): 1/3 cup (helps give structure and keeps the bhatura crisp yet soft)
  • Curd (Yogurt): 1/2 cup (preferably slightly sour)
  • Sugar: 1 tsp (helps in getting a beautiful golden-brown color)
  • Baking Soda: 1/4 tsp
  • Salt: 1/2 tsp
  • Oil:1 tbsp (for the dough) + extra for deep frying

Steps

  1. Soak the chickpeas in water overnight or for at least 7 to 8 hours.
  2. Mix the dry ingredients for the bhatura: 2 cups maida, 1/3 cup sooji, 1/2 tsp salt, 1 tsp sugar, and 1/4 tsp baking soda.
  3. Add the wet ingredients (1/2 cup curd and 1 tbsp oil) into the flour mixture and rub it together.
  4. Knead the mixture into a soft dough for 5 to 7 minutes using warm water, stretching and slapping it to activate the gluten.
  5. Rest the dough by coating it with oil, covering it with a damp cloth, and placing it in a warm spot for 1 to 2 hours.
  6. Boil the chickpeas in a pressure cooker with water, salt, 1/4 tsp baking soda, whole spices, and a tea bag for 6 to 8 whistles until perfectly tender.
